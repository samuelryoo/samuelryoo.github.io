About

About me

I am a Computer Science and Engineering student at UC Irvine. I like the layer where software meets hardware: circuit boards, microcontrollers, motors, and sensors.

Most of what I have built so far has been for robots and drones. At Refined Robotics in Tokyo I designed the power distribution for a delivery robot's sensor payload, including two circuit board revisions and the motor control for its delivery mechanism. Before that I led the electrical work on a robotic arm with ZotBotics, set up the radio and telemetry links on a multirotor, and wrote bare-metal firmware for an AVR microcontroller.

The direction I am heading is neuroengineering. A brain-computer interface needs the same things a robot does, low-noise electronics, real-time firmware, and reliable hardware, applied to much smaller signals. I joined UC Irvine's Brain-Computer Interface Lab in September 2026, and on my own time I am building an EMG-controlled hand as a first biosignal platform.

Outside engineering I cook, play golf and acoustic guitar, follow the Dodgers, and backpack in the San Gabriel Mountains. In high school I founded Making an Impact, a non-profit that organized homeless outreach and grew to more than 200 members.
